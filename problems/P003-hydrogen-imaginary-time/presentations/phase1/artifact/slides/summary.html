In short

Summary

In imaginary time the ground-state energy is the decay rate of exp(−βH). Feynman's slicing turns its trace into a closed chain of beads joined by Gaussian springs.

For the Coulomb potential each bead carries a weight that is not integrable at the nucleus. The bead integral is infinite for every m and every Δτ, and the springs cannot prevent it.

The exact kernel is finite everywhere, K₁(0,0) = 0.91957. The failure comes from the slicing: the imaginary-time Trotter formula needs a potential bounded below.

The exact trace is infinite as well, from large distances, so E₀ has to be read from the kernel at a point.

Next phase: replace the potential factor in each slice by its average over the free paths of that slice (Kole and De Raedt, 2001).

Derivation checked by six numerical checks and one external audit round. Two citations, Reed–Simon and Simon 1982, are still to be checked against the originals.

For the Coulomb potential the weight at each bead is not integrable at the nucleus, so the bead integral is infinite for every number of segments, and the springs cannot help.
