## Was the result derived or retrieved?

The user's observation at the time: "it did not really solve the problem — it
looked the solution up on the web and transcribed it, which is why its first
answer was wrong, since option C was also incorrect."

This is the workflow's central concern, observed directly on the first problem
attempted. Two things are worth separating.

The evidence for it is behavioural, not documentary. No source was cited by the
model and none was requested, so there is nothing to check. Under the current
method the model would have had to declare up front what it believed the
standard result to be and where it is presented, which would have made the
retrieval visible rather than inferred.

The tell was not the bibliography. A model transcribing a published solution
cites accurately; that is what makes citation a poor detector. The tell was that
the transcribed answer was wrong in a way the model could not see, because it
had not derived it — and it then located the error immediately once told an
option was false, which is the behaviour of something that can do the physics
but did not.
